What trading actually is

Before we look at a single chart, let's clear up what trading even means. No strategy yet — the goal is just to remove the mystery.

WHAT YOU'LL UNDERSTAND BY THE END

What is trading?Buying something now, hoping to sell it later for more (or the reverse).
Trading vs. investingDifferent time horizons, different mindsets.
What is a market?A place where people agree on a price to buy and sell something.
The different marketsStocks, forex, futures, crypto, indices, commodities.
What moves price?More buyers than sellers pushes price up, and vice versa.
Buyers vs. sellersEvery trade needs one of each.
Going long / shortBetting price goes up, or betting it goes down.
Profit and lossThe difference between what you paid and what you sold for.
Broker, exchange, platformWho lets you place the trade, and where it happens.
Leverage — and its dangerBorrowed buying power. Multiplies wins and losses.

CHAPTER 03 / 18

Reading a candlestick chart

Every strategy is really just a way of reading candles. One candle first, then a few together, then a whole sequence.

THE PARTS OF A SINGLE CANDLE

Open, High, Low, CloseThe four prices every candle records for its period.
Body vs. wickThick part is open-to-close; thin lines show extremes.
Bullish vs. bearishClosed higher, or lower — usually green vs. red.
Candle rangeFull distance from high to low.
Candle closeWhere price ended when the period finished — important later.

TIMEFRAMES

A timeframe is how much time one candle represents.

1 min→5 min→ 15 min→30 min→ 1 hour→4 hour→Daily

How price moves

Zooming out to groups of candles. LSD is built on recognizing a strong move followed by a pause.

WORDS YOU'LL USE EVERY DAY

ImpulseA fast, decisive move in one direction.
RetracementA partial pullback before the move usually continues.
ConsolidationPrice going sideways.
ExpansionCandles getting bigger.
CompressionCandles getting smaller.
RangePrice stuck between a ceiling and a floor.
BreakoutPrice escaping that ceiling or floor.
ReversalThe trend actually changing direction.
CHAPTER 05 / 18

Market structure

Where you stop describing the chart and start reading it — is it trending up, down, or nowhere?

SPOTTING A TREND

Uptrend
  • Higher High → Higher Low → repeat
  • Each peak and dip higher than the last
Downtrend
  • Lower Low → Lower High → repeat
  • Each dip and peak lower than the last

BREAK OF STRUCTURE

Structural high / lowThe peak or dip the market needs to break to prove a shift.
Breaking a high or lowPrice trading beyond that point.
Wick break vs. body breakA close beyond it is stronger proof than a wick.
Major vs. minor structureNot every high or low matters equally.
DO THIS30 random charts. Mark highs/lows, label the trend up/down/ranging. No trading yet.
CHAPTER 06 / 18

Protecting your money

Before any strategy, on purpose. It's not about being right every time — it's about losing little when you're wrong.

THE CONCEPTS THAT KEEP YOU IN THE GAME

Risk and "R"What you're willing to lose on one trade.
Stop loss / take profitAutomatic exits, decided ahead of time.
Risk / rewardWhat you stand to make vs. what you're risking.
Position sizingHow many units, based on risk — never a guess.
Win rate vs. expectancyWinning often means nothing if losses are bigger.
Losing streaks & drawdownEvery trader has them.
Loss limitsA hard stop for the day/week, set in advance.
Risk per tradeA fixed, small % of your account.

THE ONE FORMULA WORTH MEMORIZING

Expectancy = (Win Rate × Average Win) − (Loss Rate × Average Loss)

CHAPTER 07 / 18

Supply & demand zones

The first half of LSD, taught alone — no liquidity yet.

WHAT A ZONE ACTUALLY IS

Demand zone
  • Calm area, then a strong upward move away from it
  • We mark where the buying started
Supply zone
  • Calm area, then a strong downward move
  • The mirror image of demand

GOOD ZONE VS. BAD ZONE

Good
  • Strong, clear move away
  • Tight, compact origin
  • Clean candles
Bad
  • Weak, choppy move
  • Huge, vague area
  • Candles fighting each other
CHAPTER 08 / 18

Liquidity

Why does a "textbook" supply-and-demand trade so often fail right after it looks perfect? Liquidity.

THE QUESTION THAT STARTED IT ALL

Demand forms→Price returns→ A trader buys→Dips further, stops them→Reverses up

That cluster of stop losses is liquidity — understanding where it sits is what separates LSD from ordinary supply & demand trading.

WHERE LIQUIDITY TENDS TO SIT

Just above recent highs, below recent lows
Equal highs and equal lows
Swing highs and swing lows
Popular, well-known S&D zones
IMPORTANTA liquidity sweep doesn't automatically mean reversal — that's a common beginner mistake.
CHAPTER 09 / 18

Putting LSD together

You already know zones, structure, and liquidity — LSD is those three, in the right order.

LSD IN ONE SENTENCE

Strong move creates a zone→Pullback creates liquidity→ Liquidity breaks structure→Price returns to zone→Reaction is your entry

WALKING THROUGH A FULL EXAMPLE (DEMAND)

  1. 01A strong upward move happens
  2. 02You mark the demand zone it came from
  3. 03Price pulls back
  4. 04That pullback creates liquidity below a recent swing low
  5. 05Price breaks the high that created that liquidity
  6. 06Price returns toward your demand zone
  7. 07The zone is still untouched
  8. 08Price taps the zone
  9. 09It reacts there, without closing through it
  10. 10Entry confirmation, stop loss, target defined
CHAPTER 10 / 18

The non-negotiable rules

These four rules make LSD mechanical instead of a guessing game.

RULE 1 — BUILD THE ZONE CORRECTLY

Demand
  • Last down candle right before the strong upward move
Supply
  • Last up candle right before the strong downward move

RULE 2 — THE ZONE MUST STILL BE UNTOUCHED

Valid
  • Zone → liquidity → structure → return → entry, nothing touched it first
Invalid
  • A candle already dipped into the zone before your entry
RULE 3Wicks can poke into the zone. If a candle closes inside it, the zone is invalidated.

RULE 4 — THE LIQUIDITY HAS TO PROVE ITSELF

The hardest idea in the course — take your time.

For demand
  • Liquidity below → price must later break the high that created it
For supply
  • Liquidity above → price must later break the low that created it
CHAPTER 11 / 18

The A+ checklist

Not every technically-valid setup is worth trading. Here's how to be picky.

RULE 5 — HOW PRICE APPROACHES MATTERS

Good
  • Clean, direct move toward the zone
Bad / very bad
  • Choppy movement
  • Strong opposing trend forming on the way
RULE 6Keep zones small — a precise reaction area, not a giant rectangle.

RUN THROUGH THIS BEFORE EVERY TRADE

  1. 01Direction — what has recent structure been doing?
  2. 02Displacement — was there a genuinely strong move?
  3. 03Zone — drawn correctly?
  4. 04Untapped — nothing touched it yet?
  5. 05Liquidity — obvious liquidity in front of the zone?
  6. 06Liquidity validation — did it break the structure it needs to?
  7. 07Approach — moving cleanly toward the zone?
  8. 08Tap & reaction — did it reject, not close through it?
  9. 09Entry — did the trigger actually fire?
  10. 10Risk & target — within your limit, based on structure?
IF ANY BOX FAILSYou don't take the trade. Full stop.
